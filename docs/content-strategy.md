# Content Strategy

**Positioning north star:** Ayush moves across AI, software, products, and research to turn messy real-world problems into working systems. Not title-stacking. Evidence over adjectives. This site is a **digital home**, not a portfolio brochure.

Companion docs: `personal-positioning.md`, `content-style.md`, `content-fact-check.md`.

---

## 1. Audience and jobs-to-be-done

| Audience | What they need | What the site should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hiring managers / AI orgs | Credible recent work, stack honesty, communication clarity | Clear role narrative + 2–3 deep cases |
| Collaborators / founders | Judgment, taste, follow-through | Products + how you think |
| Researchers / academic contacts | Specific research claims with evidence | Research as cases or notes — no fake papers |
| Community / past orgs | Continuity of story | Grounded leadership & extracurriculars |
| General visitors | Who is this person? | Home composition + About |

**Not primary:** agency clients shopping rate cards. Services pricing is de-emphasized.

## 3. Content pillars

| Pillar | Include | Exclude / demote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Systems built** | Products, shipped projects, meaningful prototypes | Self-referential “portfolio as project” as a featured case |
| **Applied AI / research** | Problems, methods, honest outcomes | Unverified % accuracy, award lists without proof |
| **Path & craft** | Education, roles, tools in context of work | Skills clouds, cert walls, title rotators |
| **Life & community** | Specific extracurriculars with roles/dates | Inflated reach ($30K, 50K students) without evidence |
| **Presence** | Curated elsewhere links | 40+ profile dump, WhatsApp phone |

## 4. What to keep, rewrite, cut

### Keep (after verification)

- Education facts (LNCTS, IIT Madras, Seoni)  
- Employment timeline structure (FoCDoT, Outlier, RaSoR, UiPath, Salesforce, Kanha House)  
- Products: Open Framework, AyushMushrooms (honest “in progress”)  
- Stronger projects once claims cleaned  
- Extracurriculars that are specific (BachpanShala, MUN, Raahat, festivals, open mic)  
- Contact form infrastructure  

### Rewrite

- Hero / About copy → positioning narrative  
- Experience bullets → remove name-drops or clarify relationship  
- Project descriptions → problem / approach / outcome; kill marketing fluff (“visionary collective…”)  
- Research → methods + scope; metrics only if documented  
- SEO title/description → digital home language  

### Cut or hard-gate

- Vanity metrics (100% success, 24/7 support, generic CountUps)  
- Agency services + USD pricing grid  
- Phone / WhatsApp public exposure  
- Inflated leadership cards until verified  
- Duplicate About sections  
- Particle/Three.js as content substitute  
- Low-signal profile links

## 5. Content types and templates

### Home blurb (≤40 words)

One paragraph restating positioning; no titles list.

### Work card (list)

- Title  
- Type badge (Product / Project / Research / Client)  
- 1–2 sentence summary  
- Year / status  
- Link to case  

### Case study

See IA template in `information-architecture.md`. Prefer screenshots or real artifacts over gradient cards.

### Experience entry

- Role, org, dates, location/remote  
- 2–4 bullets of **what you did** (verbs + objects)  
- Optional: stack tags  
- No unverified third-party brand collaboration claims  

### Note / essay (future)

- Title, date, 1-line dek  
- Body in markdown  
- Tags: ai, product, research, community

## 6. Voice and claims policy

- **Evidence over adjectives:** Prefer “deployed a prototype with 20 farmers” only if true; else “built a prototype aimed at advisory recommendations.”  
- **No title stacking:** One primary framing at a time. Secondary skills appear in context.  
- **Status honesty:** “In progress,” “prototype,” “client site,” “course project” labeled clearly.  
- **Metrics:** Require source in `content-fact-check.md` before publishing.  
- **Privacy:** Email public; phone private.  

Full voice rules: `content-style.md`.

## 10. Success criteria for content

Content strategy succeeds when:

1. A stranger understands what Ayush does in **10 seconds** on `/`.  
2. A hiring manager finds **proof** in ≤3 clicks.  
3. No published number fails a basic “can you show the source?” test.  
4. Removing the nav still leaves a branded, non-generic first viewport.  
5. No page feels empty or like a leftover SPA section.
